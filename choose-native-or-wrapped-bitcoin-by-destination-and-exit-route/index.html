Choose native or wrapped bitcoin by destination and exit route

Choose native BTC when you need Bitcoin-chain control; use wrapped BTC only when the destination app accepts that token and its redemption route fits before sending.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Oct 16, 2026 · 18:03 UTC 3 min read

Choose native bitcoin when you need BTC on Bitcoin; choose a wrapped version when an app on another chain requires its own BTC token. A bridge moves value between networks, but the asset you receive depends on its design and the destination chain.

Ethereum.org describes common bridge designs as locking assets and minting a destination token, or burning a token before minting on another chain. For the treasury steps around a transfer, see this guide to moving crypto with a super bridge. The key decision is where you need to use or hold the bitcoin after the transfer.

What is the difference between native and wrapped bitcoin?

Native BTC is bitcoin recorded on the Bitcoin network. Wrapped BTC is a token on another network that represents bitcoin under that token’s specific backing and redemption arrangement.

With a lock-and-mint design, BTC is locked and a corresponding token is issued on the destination chain; on the return, that token is burned and BTC is released. The token may be designed to track BTC one for one, but its redemption depends on the bridge’s custody, contracts or validators. It is not a Bitcoin transaction until BTC is released on Bitcoin.

When should you choose native BTC?

Choose native BTC if your destination is a Bitcoin address or a service that accepts BTC on Bitcoin. It keeps the asset in Bitcoin’s own transaction system, rather than adding a token contract and bridge redemption path to the holding.

That choice does not make a cross-chain transfer unnecessary: if the app you want to use runs on another network, native BTC alone may not be accepted there. Check the destination network and asset format before sending. A ticker such as “BTC” in a wallet does not, by itself, tell you which chain holds the asset.

When does wrapped BTC make sense?

Use wrapped BTC when the destination application specifically supports that token on that network. It can make BTC available for uses such as trading, lending or providing liquidity on chains where native Bitcoin transactions cannot directly interact with the app.

Before bridging, check these details:

  • The destination network and exact token the app accepts.
  • How the token is backed, and who or what controls minting and redemption.
  • The route back to native BTC, including fees, processing steps and any stated minimums.
  • Whether you can access the required wallet and network to redeem or transfer the token.

How should you make the final choice?

For most holders who want BTC as bitcoin, native BTC is the simpler fit: it avoids a separate wrapped token and its redemption dependencies. Choose wrapped BTC when a specific destination use justifies those extra dependencies, and verify the token and exit route before transferring funds.
