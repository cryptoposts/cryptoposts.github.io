Ethereum Foundation doubles team for AI and quantum security

The Ethereum Foundation doubled its protocol security team and put hash-based cryptography first, citing AI as a more urgent risk than quantum computing.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Nov 25, 2026 · 13:33 UTC 2 min read

The Ethereum Foundation doubled its protocol security team as it prioritises a cryptographic overhaul, researcher Kev Wedderburn said in a November 23 Foundation announcement. Wedderburn said AI had recently emerged as “a more urgent risk” than quantum computers to Ethereum’s cryptography.

The Foundation said AI models are finding bugs in production software faster than people can review them, and are beginning to improve on decades-old algorithms. Wedderburn said that raises concern about the mathematical assumptions cryptography relies on, while cautioning that AI progress does not itself show those assumptions can be broken.

The proposed changes cover Ethereum’s consensus mechanism, user accounts and data blobs. Validators would use hash-based keys, wallets would support hash-based signatures, and blobs would switch to hash-based commitments, according to the Foundation. After more than a year evaluating candidate signature schemes, it is advocating for hash-based cryptography to replace elliptic curves; the Foundation says Ethereum already depends on hashes being secure, so the shift would not add a new security assumption.

The Foundation said formal verification and security work must keep pace with protocol development. The overhaul would require changes to BLS-based tooling, contracts, wallets, multisigs and staking pools; larger signatures could also affect hardware requirements for people running nodes. Features planned for the protocol may be delayed, the Foundation said.

At Devcon in Mumbai, Ethereum researcher Justin Drake proposed December 2028 as a target for the transition, while the Foundation’s internal deadline remains 2029, with a timeline review planned for January, Wedderburn wrote. Early specifications are underway, and the Foundation has begun iterating on a first consensus-layer devnet, the post said. Decrypt’s report also described the team expansion and the Foundation’s push to prioritise the cryptographic work.
