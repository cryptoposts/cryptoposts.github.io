How zerofi’s wrapped assets fit into DeFi

ZeroFi’s wrapped assets make deposits usable in DeFi on another chain, but their value depends on backing, redemption and the bridge that connects them.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Oct 24, 2026 · 01:54 UTC 3 min read

ZeroFi describes zAssets as ERC-20 tokens representing assets deposited on another chain, with zXMR as its example for Monero. The token can interact with Ethereum-based DeFi contracts; its holder relies on the bridge to support redemption back to XMR.

That is one kind of wrapped asset: a token issued against an underlying asset held or controlled elsewhere. For the mechanics behind zerofi, the key questions are where the backing sits, who can release it and how the token is burned or returned during redemption.

How does zerofi turn an asset into a DeFi token?

ZeroFi says a user deposits XMR and receives zXMR on Ethereum. The ERC-20 format lets DeFi contracts handle the representation as a token, so it can in principle be traded, supplied to a lending market or paired in a liquidity pool where those applications support it.

The token does not make Monero itself an ERC-20 asset. It adds a bridge layer between the deposited XMR and the transferable zXMR. A user leaving DeFi must return or burn the wrapped token under the bridge’s rules to request the underlying asset.

What roles do different wrapped assets play?

Wrapped tokens share a format, but their backing and purpose differ. Common roles include:

  • Bridged asset: represents a coin from another chain, such as the XMR-to-zXMR model ZeroFi describes; its DeFi use depends on bridge issuance and redemption.
  • Fiat-backed stablecoin: represents a claim designed to track a currency, with value tied to issuer reserves and redemption terms.
  • Liquid staking token: represents staked assets and accrued staking claims, allowing some capital to remain usable in DeFi while the underlying stake remains committed.

These are not interchangeable. A bridged token depends on cross-chain custody or control, a stablecoin on its issuer and reserves, and a liquid staking token on the staking system and its withdrawal process.

What should users check before using zAssets?

Check what backs the token, how the bridge records deposits and reserves, who authorizes withdrawals, and whether redemption is available on the chain and in the amount you need. A quoted one-to-one design describes the intended relationship; it does not by itself prove reserves or guarantee a timely withdrawal.

For most users, the practical choice is to use a wrapped asset only when its DeFi role is worth the added bridge or issuer dependency. With zerofi, that means treating zXMR as a claim mediated by the bridge, not as XMR with the same transfer and redemption properties.
