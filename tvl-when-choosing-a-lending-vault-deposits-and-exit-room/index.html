TVL when choosing a lending vault: deposits and exit room

Does TVL matter when choosing a lending vault? Somewhat: it signals depositor trust and exit room, but liquidity, utilization, allocations and concentration give a clearer view.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Nov 27, 2026 · 23:56 UTC 2 min read

TVL matters somewhat when choosing a lending vault: it signals depositor trust and potential exit room, but liquidity and allocations matter more. TVL is the dollar value of assets deposited, including funds lent to borrowers.

That distinction affects withdrawals. Borrowed funds are not available to withdraw until borrowers repay or other supply frees up, so a vault’s total can be much larger than the amount depositors can take out now.

Compare two vaults: a $200 million vault whose markets are near full utilization may offer less withdrawable liquidity than a $20 million vault with idle supply. The smaller figure can leave more room for an exit because some assets are not currently lent out.

TVL can also rise when a vault offers reward incentives. Those deposits may leave when rewards end, so a high number during an incentive period can reflect temporary demand rather than lasting depositor commitment.

Look at who holds the deposits, too. If one wallet accounts for most of a vault’s TVL, its withdrawal could drain much of the vault’s withdrawable liquidity and leave less room for other depositors to exit.

Morpho vaults allocate a single asset across lending markets and other supported yield sources, so the market list helps explain where deposits sit. Morpho vault allocations can change how much of the headline TVL is tied up in lending markets, which affects the exit room you need to assess.

In practice, open the vault details and compare available liquidity with total deposits. Then review utilization and the allocation list: high utilization means more supply is in use, while the list shows which markets or sources hold the assets.

Use TVL as a first signal, then choose the vault whose available liquidity, utilization, allocations and depositor concentration fit the amount you may need to withdraw.
