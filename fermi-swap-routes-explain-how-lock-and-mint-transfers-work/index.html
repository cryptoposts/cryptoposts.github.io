Fermi swap Routes Explain How Lock-and-Mint Transfers Work

A lock-and-mint route holds an asset on its source chain and issues a representation on another; choosing one means checking tokens, fees and the return path.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Sep 30, 2026 2 min read

Using a fermi swap lock-and-mint route means choosing a supported source asset and destination, then authorizing a deposit that backs a token representation on the destination chain. The transfer has two parts: the source chain records the lock, and the destination chain mints the corresponding representation after the bridge verifies that deposit.

Before starting, confirm the route lists the asset and chains you intend to use. The fermi swap reference belongs in the same check as the displayed token names, destination network and recipient address; similar tickers can identify different tokens. A route is useful only if the destination representation is the one your wallet or application can accept.

How does a fermi swap lock-and-mint route work?

A lock-and-mint route keeps the original token in a source-chain contract while issuing a corresponding token on another chain. The bridge’s verification process connects the two steps: after it recognizes the source deposit, it permits the destination mint.

This differs from a transfer that releases existing destination liquidity. A minted representation depends on the bridge’s records and controls, as well as the source asset held in custody. Its usefulness therefore depends on whether the destination token is accepted by the applications you plan to use and whether the bridge can process a return transfer.

What should you check before sending?

Read the route details before approving a transaction. Check the network, token contract or identity, estimated amount after fees, minimums, and any stated limits or waiting period. The amount arriving can differ from the amount sent because a route may charge fees or apply a conversion.

  • Match the source network and token to the asset in your wallet.
  • Check the destination network and receiving address before signing.
  • Review the quoted output, fee and any minimum or transfer limit.
  • Confirm the destination token is usable where you intend to send it next.

Then connect the wallet for the source network, select the route, enter an amount and review the transaction request. If the token requires a separate spending approval, that approval authorizes the contract to move the specified asset; it is distinct from submitting the deposit. Check the wallet’s network and transaction details before signing each request.

How do you complete the transfer and return?

After the source transaction confirms, keep its transaction record and follow the route status until the destination representation appears. Confirmation on the source chain does not itself put tokens in the destination wallet; the bridge’s verification and mint still have to complete.

To return, select a route that burns the representation on the destination chain and releases the corresponding original asset on the source chain. Check the return network, asset and fees independently: a route that supports a transfer in one direction may not offer the reverse route under the same conditions.

The practical choice is to use the route only when the destination token and return path suit the next step. A lower displayed fee does not settle the question if the representation is unsupported or returning it adds friction. Review the route’s live terms before sending, and start with an amount that fits its stated limits.
