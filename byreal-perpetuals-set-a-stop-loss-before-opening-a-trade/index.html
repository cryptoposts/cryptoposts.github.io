Byreal perpetuals: set a stop loss before opening a trade

Byreal lets traders attach take-profit and stop-loss levels to perpetual orders; choose the market, set direction and size, then check the trigger and margin.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Oct 21, 2026 · 22:15 UTC 2 min read

To open a Byreal perpetual with a stop loss, choose a market and attach the stop-loss level to a market or limit order before submitting it. Byreal’s perps use Hyperliquid, according to Byreal’s documentation, and its published trading tools support take-profit and stop-loss settings on both order types. The byreal interface lists perpetuals under “Perps.”

How do you set a stop loss on a Byreal perpetual?

Select “Perps,” choose the contract, then set whether the position is long or short and enter its size. In the order panel, set the stop-loss price alongside the order; Byreal’s published perps tooling also allows a stop loss to be added to an existing position.

For a long position, the stop generally sits below the entry price; for a short, it generally sits above. The trigger should reflect the price level at which you want the position to start closing. Review the order summary before confirming, and check that the stop is attached to the position you intend to protect.

Should you use a market or limit order?

A market order prioritizes opening the position promptly, while a limit order specifies the entry price and may remain unfilled. Byreal’s tooling supports attaching a stop to either type, but a stop does not guarantee a particular exit price: execution can differ from the trigger during fast price moves or thin liquidity.

  • Choose the contract and check its trading terms.
  • Set long or short, order type, size and entry price if using a limit order.
  • Enter the stop-loss trigger and check it against your position direction.
  • Review margin and liquidation details, then confirm and verify the stop appears with the position.

What should you check after opening the trade?

Confirm that the order filled and that the stop is active for the resulting position. A pending limit order may not create an open position until it fills, so check the position and its attached risk controls after execution rather than assuming the stop is already protecting an unfilled order.

Byreal says its perpetual contracts offer leverage that varies by market. Higher leverage leaves less room for adverse price movement before liquidation, so the stop should be set with the liquidation level and available margin in view. A stop-loss automates an exit trigger; it cannot remove execution risk or prevent liquidation if the position reaches that threshold first.
