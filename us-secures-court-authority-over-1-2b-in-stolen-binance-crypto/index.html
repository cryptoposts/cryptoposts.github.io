US secures court authority over $1.2B in stolen Binance crypto

The Justice Department secured court authority over more than $1.2 billion of Binance’s stolen crypto, part of $2.7 billion frozen as recovery efforts continue.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Nov 15, 2026 · 20:11 UTC 2 min read

The Justice Department obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, it said Thursday, Nov. 12. The amount is part of more than $2.7 billion frozen through U.S. government and private-sector efforts, according to the Justice Department’s announcement.

The FBI says North Korean military members, whom it calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or around Oct. 29. The bureau attributed the theft to North Korea in an FBI alert issued Oct. 31.

The Justice Department said it worked with virtual asset service providers to interdict the stolen funds. It said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are continuing as the attackers move them through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges.

The court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of the frozen total; the department did not say the full amount had been transferred to government-controlled wallets. The distinction matters because seizure or restraint is a legal step, while the government may still need to pursue forfeiture and resolve claims to recovered funds.

The FBI’s attribution names TraderTraitor, while the Justice Department describes the group as members of the North Korean military. The announcement gives no breakdown of the assets covered by the court authorization or timeline for forfeiture proceedings.
