World Bank to explore USDC rails with Citi and Circle

The World Bank Group will study USDC payment rails with Citi and Circle, exploring whether they can speed fund disbursements and improve tracking.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Nov 22, 2026 · 00:37 UTC 2 min read

The World Bank Group said on Nov. 17 it will explore regulated stablecoin payment rails with Citi and Circle, including whether they can improve the speed, cost and transparency of payments to its clients. The World Bank Group announcement says the work will examine U.S. dollar-denominated stablecoins alongside or as an improvement to its existing correspondent banking arrangements for fund disbursements.

Citi’s banking connections and Circle’s payment infrastructure are central to the proposed model, according to the World Bank Group. Circle’s infrastructure would mint and settle funds in USDC, while the initial exploration will focus on an intermediated, interoperable model. The group said it will engage borrower countries where digital asset use is supported and permitted.

The initiative builds on FundsChain, the World Bank Group’s blockchain platform for tracking disbursements across investment projects in developing countries. The group says more than 350 projects in 96 countries are onboarded to its digital ledgers, where project participants can see how funds are used.

World Bank Group President Ajay Banga said the effort is intended to examine whether faster, lower-cost movement of money can support development work. The announcement describes the project as an exploration; it does not announce a launch date or say that stablecoins will replace current payment arrangements.

The World Bank Group said it may explore working with other banks over time to broaden the initiative. Citi and Circle are the named partners in the initial work, which links traditional banking rails with stablecoin settlement, according to the group’s announcement.
