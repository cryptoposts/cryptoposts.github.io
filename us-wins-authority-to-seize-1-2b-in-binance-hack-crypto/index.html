US wins authority to seize $1.2B in Binance hack crypto

The Justice Department says a court authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion of crypto stolen from Binance, within $2.7 billion frozen so far.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Nov 16, 2026 · 00:39 UTC 2 min read

On Nov. 12, the Justice Department said it had court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The Associated Press reported that the FBI described the funds as seized; the department’s announcement specifies that the authorization covers seizure or restraint.

The $1.2 billion is part of more than $2.7 billion in stolen assets frozen so far through U.S. government and private-sector efforts, according to the Justice Department’s announcement. The department said the theft involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets taken from Binance on or about Oct. 29. The AP put the theft at about $6.3 billion.

The FBI attributed the attack to members of the North Korean military and calls the activity “TraderTraitor,” according to the Justice Department. The FBI’s Oct. 31 alert also attributed the theft to North Korea and said the attackers were converting the stolen assets primarily into ether.

The court authorization does not mean all the stolen funds have been recovered. The Justice Department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are continuing, as the attackers move funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. The more than $2.7 billion frozen includes the amount covered by the authorization; it is not an additional recovery figure.

For customers and other potential claimants, the next step is the legal process. The AP reported that U.S. authorities typically file a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department has not said in its announcement how much of the authorized amount has been transferred to government-controlled wallets or when any forfeiture claims will be filed.
