Fluidkey Says SigNoz Flaws Led to $1.6 Million Theft

Fluidkey says two unpatched SigNoz flaws exposed session tokens used to steal about $1.6 million from 52 accounts, while personal data and viewing keys were accessed.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Nov 23, 2026 · 10:31 UTC 2 min read

Fluidkey said an attacker used two unpatched flaws in its SigNoz logging tool to steal about $1.6 million from 52 accounts on Nov. 19. The company’s incident report says unauthorized transfers occurred between 17:28 and 18:05 UTC, across Ethereum, Base, BNB Chain, Polygon, Optimism and Arbitrum.

Fluidkey said its self-hosted SigNoz installation was running version 0.121.1. The attacker exploited two vulnerabilities to read internal logs between Nov. 12 and 19; fixes were available in versions 0.141.1 and 0.142.1. Some services had recorded user session tokens and viewing keys for recently registered accounts in those logs.

According to The Defiant’s report, the stolen tokens let the attacker create transfer requests, mark them approved and raise two-factor spending limits. Fluidkey said the attacker sent small payments to trigger notifications; when users opened and unlocked the app, it signed the prepared transfers.

Fluidkey said users’ private spending keys remained on their devices and were not compromised. But the app’s signing process allowed the prepared transfers to execute when users unlocked it. The company reported that logs also exposed personal data linked to 70 accounts, including 38 email addresses, 37 names, 17 IBANs, 24 postal addresses, four dates of birth and two phone numbers. Viewing keys for 54 newly registered accounts were accessed; Fluidkey says those keys reveal account addresses, balances and transaction history, but cannot move funds.

Fluidkey took transaction processing offline at 18:05 UTC on Nov. 19 and restored service the next day with version 1.79.22, it said. Before relaunch, it updated SigNoz, rotated credentials, ended existing sessions and changed its servers so a session token alone could no longer create and approve a transfer request. The company said it had contacted affected users and was tracing the funds with investigators; its report says the investigation is ongoing.
