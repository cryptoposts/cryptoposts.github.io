US wins seizure authority for $1.2B in Binance theft

The Justice Department says a court authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion of crypto stolen from Binance as efforts to trace the funds continue.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Nov 19, 2026 · 19:54 UTC 2 min read

U.S. authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday, Nov. 12. The department’s announcement says the amount is part of a larger pool of stolen assets frozen through government and private-sector efforts.

Those efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion in total, according to the Justice Department. It said the theft took place on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets; the FBI identifies the North Korean military actors involved as TraderTraitor.

The court authorization applies to more than $1.2 billion of the frozen funds. The department said it obtained authority to “seize or restrain” the assets, so the announcement does not say that all of them have been transferred into government custody. The Associated Press report said the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture efforts are continuing, the Justice Department said. TraderTraitor actors are laundering assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and the FBI has urged virtual-asset service providers to block transactions connected to addresses used to move the Binance funds.
