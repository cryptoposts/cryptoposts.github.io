Mantle bridge swaps have no fixed slippage limit

A Mantle bridge transfer can preserve the same token amount, but a route that swaps assets can deliver less as prices move or pool liquidity thins.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Oct 16, 2026 · 08:49 UTC 3 min read

A Mantle bridge swap has no universal slippage limit: the amount that can change depends on the route and the tolerance set before signing. A standard bridge transfer moves a token between networks; a swap route also trades it, so the swap’s price impact and fees can reduce what arrives.

The distinction matters when comparing routes or reading an estimate. This mantle bridge guide explains route choices in more detail; here, the key question is whether the transfer includes a trade.

Does a Mantle bridge transfer have slippage?

A canonical bridge transfer does not use a trading pool to exchange one asset for another, so it has no swap price slippage. It carries the mapped asset across networks; network gas and any stated fees affect the net value, but they are separate from slippage.

A route that changes tokens, or uses a liquidity provider to deliver funds on another chain, may include one or more swaps. Each trade can move the execution price, and a multi-step route can accumulate price impact across its legs. The quote is an estimate at that moment, not a promise of a fixed dollar value.

How much can a bridge swap amount slip?

There is no standard percentage for every Mantle route. The practical limit is usually the route’s slippage tolerance: it sets how far the output may fall below the quoted minimum before the transaction fails. A tighter tolerance protects the minimum output but can cause a transaction to revert if prices move before execution.

For example, if a route quotes 100 tokens and the configured tolerance is 1%, its minimum output would be about 99 tokens before considering separately listed fees. That is an illustration of the setting, not a forecast; pool depth, trade size, token volatility and routing determine the actual quote and execution.

What should you check before signing?

Compare the amount you expect to receive after route fees, then check the minimum output in the wallet or route screen. A low tolerance is useful when the quote is stable and the route can execute quickly; a higher tolerance may let a trade through during movement, while accepting a worse rate.

  • Check whether the route only bridges the same token or also swaps it.
  • Review the quoted output, minimum output, and any bridge or provider fee.
  • Reduce the input or wait for a better quote if the minimum output is unacceptable.

If execution would fall below the minimum, the swap generally reverts rather than silently delivering less; the submitted transaction can still incur network gas. Judge the route by the minimum amount you are willing to accept, not by its headline quote.
