Blockchain.com and NYSE explore tokenized stock access

Blockchain.com and NYSE signed a plan to explore tokenized stock access through a digital venue, while sharing market data across their platforms.

The Crypto Newsline DeskSep 24, 20262 min read

Blockchain.com and NYSE Group signed a memorandum of understanding on Sept. 23 to explore giving Blockchain.com users access to tokenized U.S.-listed stocks and ETFs through NYSE’s planned digital trading venue, subject to regulatory approvals. The Blockchain.com announcement says the proposal targets 24-hour trading and pairs securities access with market data shared between the companies.

How would Blockchain.com users reach the stocks?

The companies propose distributing tokenized versions of NYSE-listed equities and exchange-traded funds through NYSE’s digital alternative trading system, or ATS, according to the announcement. The agreement is exploratory: the companies have not announced a launch date, and access depends on required regulatory approvals.

Blockchain.com said the service could reach tens of millions of users around the world. The company’s announcement also says it has more than 44 million confirmed accounts, but that figure does not indicate how many users would qualify for the proposed product.

The arrangement follows Blockchain.com’s existing tokenized-stock offering in Europe. Reuters reported that the company had begun selling tokenized stocks to European customers, but said Blockchain.com had not disclosed how many customers had bought them. Reuters also reported that the companies had not yet named the countries where the planned NYSE-linked products would be available.

What would the data agreement add?

The MOU also outlines a two-way data arrangement. NYSE affiliate ICE Data Services plans to distribute Blockchain.com’s crypto market data and analytics to its subscribing clients, while Blockchain.com plans to add certain ICE and NYSE feeds to its app, according to the company’s announcement.

Blockchain.com says the feeds would put real-time stock information in front of its more than 44 million confirmed accounts. The companies have not specified which feeds would be included or when they would appear in the app.

What rights would token holders have?

The announcement does not detail the legal rights attached to the planned tokens. Reuters reported that blockchain-based stock tokens often do not give buyers the same rights as traditional shares and typically do not make them shareholders in the underlying company.

That distinction matters because the product would represent access to tokenized securities, while the exact structure and investor protections have not been set out publicly. Reuters also reported that the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission had recently unveiled an exemption for platforms selling blockchain-based versions of stocks and other securities.

For now, the companies say they will explore the distribution plan and data links. Any service launch remains contingent on required regulatory approvals; neither company has announced a launch date or eligible countries, according to Reuters and the companies’ announcement.
