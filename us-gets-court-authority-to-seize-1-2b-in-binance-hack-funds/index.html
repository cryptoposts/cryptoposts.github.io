US Gets Court Authority to Seize $1.2B in Binance Hack Funds

The Justice Department says courts authorized seizure or restraint of $1.2 billion from Binance's $6 billion hack; tracing and forfeiture efforts continue.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Nov 23, 2026 · 04:32 UTC 2 min read

The Justice Department said Nov. 12 that US authorities had court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The department’s announcement puts the amount within a wider effort that has frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets.

The FBI said the theft took place on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. The agency attributed the attack to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor, according to the Justice Department. The department did not say that all of the $1.2 billion had been transferred into government custody; its announcement describes the court authorization as covering assets to “seize or restrain.”

US government agencies and private-sector partners worked with virtual-asset service providers to freeze the funds, the department said. It named the Justice Department’s National Security and Criminal divisions, the US Attorney’s Office for the District of Columbia, the FBI, the Secret Service and the IRS Criminal Investigation unit among those involved.

Tracing and recovery work is continuing, the department said, as TraderTraitor actors move assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. The FBI had issued a cyber alert urging virtual-asset providers to block transactions involving addresses linked to the laundering of the Binance funds.

The court authorization is one step in the recovery process. The Associated Press report said the US government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered assets. The Justice Department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the Binance funds remain underway.
