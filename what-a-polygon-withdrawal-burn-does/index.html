What a Polygon withdrawal burn does

A Polygon withdrawal burn destroys the bridged token on Polygon; after a validator checkpoint, an Ethereum claim releases the matching asset held in escrow.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Oct 3, 2026 · 22:13 UTC 2 min read

A Polygon PoS bridge withdrawal burns the bridged token on Polygon, then releases the matching asset on Ethereum after a checkpoint and claim. Polygon’s support guide describes this as burning the Polygon representation and unlocking the Ethereum tokens; it is not a fee charged for using Polygon.

On a deposit, the bridge locks the original asset in an Ethereum contract and creates a matching representation on Polygon. The withdrawal reverses that path: the representation is removed from circulation on Polygon so the same asset can be released from the Ethereum contract.

What does the burn prove?

The burn transaction records that the bridged tokens were destroyed on Polygon. Polygon’s bridge system uses that transaction as evidence for the later Ethereum claim, so the burn alone does not put the original asset in the user’s Ethereum wallet.

The transaction hash is the reference for following the withdrawal. If a transfer stalls or its next step is unclear, this guide to choosing a Polygon Bridge route after a stalled transfer covers the route decision in more detail.

What happens after the burn?

Polygon’s PoS withdrawal has three stages: burn the asset, wait for validators to checkpoint the relevant Polygon block on Ethereum, then submit an exit claim on Ethereum. The checkpoint commits to Polygon blocks, and the claim supplies proof that the burn is included in one of them.

  • Burn: Submit the withdrawal on Polygon; the bridged token is destroyed there.
  • Checkpoint: Wait for validators to record the block containing the burn on Ethereum.
  • Claim: Submit the proof on Ethereum so the bridge can release the escrowed asset.

The burn and claim are separate transactions on separate networks. The burn uses Polygon for gas; the claim uses Ethereum gas, and it cannot be completed until the checkpoint is available.

Can a withdrawal burn be reversed?

Polygon says a withdrawal cannot be cancelled once initiated: the burned tokens cannot be restored to the Polygon wallet. Its support guidance says a pending withdrawal can be completed later, with no time limit, so a delayed checkpoint or claim does not require starting another burn.

For most users, the practical point is to treat the burn as the start of a two-chain withdrawal, not as a completed transfer. Keep the transaction hash, wait for the checkpoint, and submit the Ethereum claim when it becomes available.
