World Bank to explore USDC payments with Citi and Circle

The World Bank Group will study whether Citi's banking links and Circle's USDC infrastructure can improve development fund payments across eligible borrower countries.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Nov 20, 2026 · 16:34 UTC 2 min read

The World Bank Group said on November 17 it will explore using regulated dollar stablecoins for fund disbursements with Citi and Circle, according to its announcement. Citi would connect the model to established banking rails, while Circle would provide infrastructure to mint and settle funds in USDC.

The initiative will assess whether stablecoin payment rails could work alongside or improve the Group’s existing disbursement arrangements, which rely on correspondent banking. The World Bank said the initial exploration will focus on an intermediated, interoperable model; it has not announced a launch date, participating countries or payment amounts.

Which borrower countries could take part?

The World Bank said it will engage borrower countries where digital asset use is supported and permitted. It also said it may later work with other banks to broaden the initiative.

The work builds on FundsChain, the Group’s blockchain platform for tracking disbursements across investment projects. The World Bank says more than 350 projects in 96 countries are on its digital ledgers, where project participants can see how funds are used.

How could the payment model change disbursements?

FundsChain tracks the use of funds; the new exploration considers whether stablecoins could also be used in the payment and settlement process. The World Bank has framed the work as a study of how US dollar stablecoins and blockchain payment rails might improve cost-efficiency, speed and transparency.

The Block reported on the announcement that the proposed rails would be considered alongside traditional correspondent banking. No live disbursements or measured savings have been announced; the initiative’s stated aim is to assess whether this setup can improve existing arrangements.
