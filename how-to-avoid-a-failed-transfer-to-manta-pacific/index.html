How to avoid a failed transfer to Manta Pacific

Most failed Manta Pacific transfers come from the wrong source chain, missing native gas, or an unfinished claim step — check these before you bridge.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Oct 22, 2026 · 20:11 UTC 3 min read

A transfer to Manta Pacific fails when you skip one of three checks: the source chain, the gas balance, or the claim step. Manta Pacific is an Ethereum Layer-2 network, so funds move to it through the official Manta bridge or a bridge aggregator. The bridge page explains what Manta Bridge is and its usage; the failure points are the same on any route.

Why do Manta Pacific transfers fail?

Most failures trace back to one of three causes: a deposit from an unsupported chain, a token the bridge does not list, or a claim step that never runs. On many routes that claim needs ETH in the Manta Pacific wallet; with no balance there, the transfer stays pending.

The bridge UI shows warnings for some of these before you confirm, but not all. A transaction can also revert on the source chain when your wallet's ETH balance is too low to cover the approval and bridge fees.

For an ERC-20 token, the first transaction is an approval of the bridge contract. If that approval is missing, the deposit transaction reverts and your funds stay in your wallet.

What should you check before you bridge?

Confirm these four things before you sign any transfer. Each one corresponds to a common failure point, and each takes seconds to verify.

  • Source chain: it must match where your tokens actually are, usually Ethereum mainnet for the official bridge's main route.
  • Token support: if the token does not appear in the destination list, the bridge will not create a claimable balance for it.
  • Destination address: use the same wallet address on Manta Pacific; a deposit to a contract address from another chain does not auto-forward.
  • Gas: keep ETH on the source chain for approval and bridge fees, and, if the route requires it, a small ETH balance on Manta Pacific for the claim.

A transfer sent from the wrong network fails before it reaches the bridge, so confirm the wallet's active network first.

What should you do if a transfer stalls?

A pending bridge transaction is not necessarily lost; it usually means the claim step has not been executed. Open the bridge's status page, find your deposit transaction hash, and run the claim if it is available.

A failed bridge transfer rarely loses funds; it either never leaves the source wallet or sits unclaimed on the destination chain.

If the source transaction reverted, stop and read the error before retrying. The rule that covers most cases: treat the claim step as part of the transfer, not as a follow-up.
