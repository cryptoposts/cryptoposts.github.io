Five checks before choosing a bridge or exchange withdrawal

Before moving tokens, compare networks, asset contracts, fees, limits, settlement time and recovery options; the cheapest route is not always the safest fit.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Sep 29, 2026 3 min read

Before sending crypto, check the networks, the exact asset, the total cost, the settlement path and the recovery options. A bridge moves assets between blockchains, while an exchange withdrawal sends tokens from a custodial account to an address on a selected network; the right route depends on where the funds need to end up.

Are the sending and receiving networks correct?

Choose a route that supports the network your destination address uses. The same token name can exist on several chains, and an address format that looks familiar does not prove that a network is supported at the receiving end.

For an exchange withdrawal, read the network label beside the asset and compare it with the deposit network shown by the recipient. For a bridge, confirm both the source and destination chains before connecting a wallet or approving a transaction. If either side is wrong, recovery may depend on the receiving service and may not be available.

Bridge status can also involve more than the source transaction. For a closer look at what keeps Mantle Bridge transfers pending, check how a transfer can wait after it has been submitted. A pending display does not by itself tell you whether the source transaction failed or the destination step is still processing.

Is it the same asset on both sides?

Compare the asset’s contract address and how it is issued on each chain, rather than relying on its ticker alone. A bridge may lock an asset on one chain and issue a representation on another, or use a different route; the receiving asset may not be interchangeable in every app.

Check the receiving wallet or service for the token contract it recognizes. For a bridge, review the route’s stated mechanism and what asset arrives. For an exchange, confirm the deposit page accepts that token on the chosen network. A mismatch can leave funds unsupported even when the transaction itself completes.

What will the transfer cost, and how long can it take?

Compare the full cost and the likely settlement path before choosing. A bridge can charge a service fee as well as source-chain gas, while an exchange may set a withdrawal fee, minimum amount or temporary network restriction; the figures and availability depend on the route and provider.

Look for the amount the recipient will receive after fees, not just the headline fee. A route with a lower listed charge may require a separate transaction to use the received asset, adding cost and steps. Check whether the provider says the network is under maintenance and whether it gives an estimated processing time.

Can you verify the destination and recover a mistake?

Check the destination address, network and any required memo or tag before sending, then keep the transaction record. A wrong address or omitted memo can make recovery difficult, and a completed blockchain transaction generally cannot be reversed by the sender.

  • Network: Match the sending and receiving chains.
  • Asset: Verify the token contract or accepted deposit version.
  • Cost: Compare fees, minimums and the amount delivered.
  • Timing and recovery: Check processing status, address details and support options.

For most users, the better choice is the route that delivers the required asset on the required network with clear fees and a verifiable status. Use a small test transfer when the amount justifies the extra fee, especially when sending to a new address or across a new route.
