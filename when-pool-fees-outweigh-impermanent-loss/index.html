When pool fees outweigh impermanent loss

Pool fees cover impermanent loss only when your earned fees exceed your position’s shortfall versus holding the same tokens, after costs and rewards.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Oct 20, 2026 · 15:01 UTC 3 min read

Pool fees cover impermanent loss when the fees you earn exceed your position’s shortfall versus holding the same tokens. That break-even point depends on how far the token prices diverge, how much trading the pool attracts and your share of its liquidity.

What does impermanent loss measure?

Impermanent loss is the value gap between your pool position and keeping the deposited tokens in your wallet. In a standard 50/50 constant-product pool, the pool continually adjusts its token mix as traders swap, leaving the provider with less of the asset that rises and more of the one that falls.

QuickSwap’s documentation explains how trading fees accrue to liquidity providers according to their contribution. For a plain-language guide to how QuickSwap swaps and pools work, see the separate explainer; the pool’s fee rules determine what share reaches providers.

How much fee income makes a position break even?

Compare the position’s value at exit, including fees, with the value of the same starting tokens held outside the pool. Fees cover impermanent loss once that first value is at least as high as the second, after transaction costs and any other expenses.

For a simple 50/50 constant-product pool with no fees, a twofold change in the relative price of one token creates about a 5.7% shortfall versus holding. If the hold portfolio would be worth $1,500 at exit, the gap is about $86, so the provider needs at least that much in fees to catch up, before costs.

What determines how quickly fees cover the gap?

Fee income rises with eligible trading volume and your share of active liquidity. The fee rate alone is not enough: a high rate on a quiet pool can produce less income than a lower rate on a busy one.

  • Estimate the pool’s trading volume over your expected holding period.
  • Apply the fee rate and your share of the liquidity that actually earns fees.
  • Compare estimated fees with the position’s shortfall against holding, then subtract costs.

Concentrated-liquidity positions need extra care: fees depend on whether the position remains in range, and the simple 50/50 formula does not describe every pool design. Check the pool’s rules and estimate your position’s fee share rather than relying on a headline yield.

Does impermanent loss disappear if prices recover?

The gap can shrink if the relative price returns toward its starting point, but “impermanent” does not mean temporary on a schedule. At withdrawal, judge the result against holding: the useful test is whether realized fees have covered the value gap and costs.
