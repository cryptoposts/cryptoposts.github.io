After buying a Solana token, choose the right tracker

DexScreener vs Birdeye for tracking a token after buying: use DexScreener for one pool and Birdeye to monitor several holdings in a wallet.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Nov 21, 2026 · 14:51 UTC 3 min read

DexScreener suits tracking one token’s pool after buying, while Birdeye suits watching several holdings in a wallet because it adds a wallet-level portfolio view.

A rugcheck reviews a token for warning signs; it does not tell you which tracker fits your position. A token can trade in several pools, and rugcheck LP locked refers to whether liquidity in a pool is locked. Keep the rugcheck separate from ongoing price and portfolio tracking.

DexScreener organizes each decentralized exchange pool as its own pair page, with a chart, price, liquidity, volume and transactions. Its pair-focused view suits a buyer who wants to follow one token’s trading activity and set alerts for that pair.

That pool distinction matters when a token trades on Raydium in more than one pool. A DexScreener watchlist follows the pair you save, so save the pool holding the most liquidity if you want the clearest view of the main market. Liquidity shows how much is available in that pool; volume and transactions show trading activity there.

Birdeye shows the token holdings associated with a public wallet address when you paste it, without connecting the wallet. Its portfolio view is useful when you want one place to follow several Solana tokens, alongside individual token pages.

For one pair, DexScreener makes liquidity and pair activity easier to keep in view, with alerts attached to that market. For a portfolio, Birdeye saves you from opening a separate pair page for every token, though its wallet view is not a substitute for checking a specific pool’s liquidity.

Neither tracker reruns a risk check. If liquidity or token holders change, review the token on rugcheck again, then use the tracker that matches what you need to monitor.

Choose DexScreener when you are tracking one token’s pool; choose Birdeye when you are tracking several tokens held in one wallet. The deciding factor is whether you want a pair-level view or a wallet-level view.

Questions readers ask next

How do I set a price alert on DexScreener?

Open the token’s pair page on DexScreener and use its alert option for that pair. Make sure it is the pool you saved, since alerts follow the selected market.

Is pasting my wallet address into Birdeye safe?

Pasting a public address does not connect your wallet or authorize transactions. The address can reveal the holdings associated with it, so treat that portfolio information as public.

Which liquidity drop should make me recheck a token?

There is no single drop that fits every pool. Recheck on rugcheck when liquidity changes sharply or alongside a change in holders, and compare the pool’s current liquidity with its own recent level.
