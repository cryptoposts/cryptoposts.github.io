How to refinance a DeFi loan without a liquidation

To refinance a DeFi loan to a lower interest rate, compare rates at your debt size, move collateral in chunks or one transaction, then confirm the old loan is closed.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Nov 26, 2026 · 02:50 UTC 3 min read

To refinance a DeFi loan to a lower rate, compare the borrow cost at your loan size, repay the old debt, move its collateral and borrow the same asset elsewhere. Start with an open loan on protocol A; finish when its debt and collateral are zero and the new position shows a lower APY.

Compare Ethereum markets on Aave, Compound, Morpho and SparkLend, including the maximum LTV each offers for your collateral. SparkLend is a permissionless money market built on the Aave V3 codebase; its market setup adds context for assessing it as a destination.

Have funds and a safe route ready

Have ETH for gas and either the debt asset in your wallet or a migration tool such as DeFi Saver. A one-transaction migration can use a flash loan to repay the old position and open the new one; manual chunks let you move collateral gradually.

Compare the new market’s borrow APY at the amount you plan to borrow, not just its current headline rate. Your own loan raises utilization. On Aave V3 markets, variable rates follow a two-slope curve that rises sharply above optimal utilization, so a large borrow can push the rate past the kink and wipe out the saving. Aave’s documentation describes the curve; DefiLlama Yields can help compare rates across protocols. Rates can change every block, so a lower variable rate is not locked in.

Move the debt and confirm the new position

Repaying debt is what frees collateral to move. Keep the health factor (HF) above about 1.5 on both positions during a chunked migration.

  1. Compare rates and LTVs. Check the new APY at your intended loan size and the destination’s maximum LTV for your collateral. The result is a destination that can support the debt without pushing the position too close to liquidation.
  2. Repay the old loan fully or in part. Use wallet funds or a migration tool. The result is less debt on protocol A and collateral freed for withdrawal.
  3. Withdraw the freed collateral. Move only the amount released by that repayment. The result is collateral available to supply on the new protocol.
  4. Supply and enable collateral. Deposit that collateral on the new protocol and mark it as collateral. The result is borrowing capacity in the new position.
  5. Borrow, then repeat as needed. Borrow the same debt asset and use it to repay more of the old loan. Keep both HFs above about 1.5 as you repeat the loop. SparkLend’s DAI and USDS borrow rates follow a governance-set Sky rate, so they move less with utilization than other markets, according to Spark documentation.
  6. Close out and verify. Finish when the old position shows zero debt and zero collateral, and the new position shows the same debt asset at a lower borrow APY.

Common refinancing questions

How do I compare collateral LTVs across protocols before moving a loan?

Compare each protocol’s maximum LTV for your specific collateral and debt asset, then check whether your planned borrow fits with room for price moves. A higher maximum LTV does not guarantee a safer position.

Can a debt swap change which asset I owe?

Yes. A debt swap exchanges your borrowed asset, but this migration keeps the same debt asset. A different asset adds exchange-rate and price risk to the move.
