Bridge relayers earn user fees for completed deliveries

Who pays bridge relayers and why? Users fund each transfer fee, covering destination gas plus a margin so messages keep moving when gas rises.

Crypto Bulletin Newsroom Nov 21, 2026 · 08:15 UTC 2 min read

A bridge transfer in progress is usually paid for by its user: the quoted fee covers destination-chain gas and a relayer’s margin. The relayer spends gas to submit a message or proof on the destination chain, then earns the fee for doing that work.

LayerZero quotes and collects its fee on the source chain. That fee covers verification and Executor delivery, including the relayer’s destination-chain gas cost. For a Bitcoin transfer, the teleswap app puts that cost in context: TeleSwap’s bridge fee covers the relayer submitting the Bitcoin proof and paying EVM gas.

The transfer fee funds verification and delivery

In LayerZero, a user pays the quoted fee before the message leaves the source chain. Part pays for verification; another part pays the Executor to deliver the message on the destination chain.

The relayer fronts the destination gas when it submits the message or proof. The fee must cover that outlay and leave enough margin to make the work worthwhile. Without an economic reason to submit, a relayer can wait.

Some protocols add token rewards to fees, or operate their own relayers to support reliable delivery. Those arrangements change who funds the incentive, but the relayer still needs compensation for the gas and work involved.

Higher gas can delay an underpriced transfer

When destination gas rises, a fee quoted earlier may no longer cover the cost of submission. A relayer that would lose money by delivering immediately may wait until the transfer is worth submitting. That is why an underpriced transfer can sit pending during a gas spike.

Say you send a cross-chain transfer and destination gas jumps before delivery. The relayer must pay the new gas cost out of pocket; if the fee and margin do not cover it, waiting can be the rational choice. The practical point is that the source-chain quote funds a later action whose cost can change.

On TeleSwap, the fee connects the Bitcoin proof submission with the EVM gas needed to complete the bridge step. When comparing routes, check the quoted fee and delivery terms before sending; that helps you judge whether the transfer price accounts for the relayer’s destination-chain work.
